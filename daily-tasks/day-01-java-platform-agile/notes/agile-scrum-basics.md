# Agile & Scrum Basics

## 1. Agile?
Agile is an iterative approach to software development and project management. Instead of delivering a massive project all at once (Waterfall), Agile focuses on delivering small, usable pieces of software incrementally.

## 2. Scrum?
Scrum is the most popular framework for implementing Agile. It provides specific roles, ceremonies, and artifacts to help teams work together.

## 3. Scrum Roles
*   **Product Owner:** Represents the business/customer. Prioritizes the work (Product Backlog).
*   **Scrum Master:** The facilitator and coach. Removes blockers and ensures the team follows Scrum practices.
*   **Development Team:** The cross-functional group of professionals building the product.

## 4. Scrum Ceremonies
*   **Sprint:** A time-boxed iteration (usually 1-4 weeks) during which a usable increment of the product is built.
*   **Sprint Planning:** A meeting at the start of the Sprint to decide what work will be done.
*   **Daily Standup (Daily Scrum):** A short, 15-minute daily meeting where team members answer:
    *   What did I do yesterday?
    *   What will I do today?
    *   Are there any blockers?
*   **Sprint Review:** A meeting at the end of the Sprint to demonstrate the completed work to stakeholders.
*   **Sprint Retrospective:** A reflection meeting to discuss what went well, what didn't, and how to improve in the next Sprint.

## 5. Artifacts & Estimation
*   **User Story:** A short, simple description of a feature told from the perspective of the user.
    *   *Format:* As a [user persona], I want [action] so that [benefit/value].
*   **Story Points:** A measure of effort, complexity, and risk associated with implementing a User Story. Teams often use the Fibonacci sequence (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) for estimation.
*   **Definition of Done (DoD):** A shared agreement of what must be completed for a User Story to be considered finished (e.g., code written, unit tests passed, code reviewed, deployed to test environment).
